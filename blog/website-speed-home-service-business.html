Is Your Website Too Slow? A Page Speed Check for Home Service Owners

Published November 5, 2026 · 5 min read

Picture someone with a flooded kitchen or a dead AC, standing in the driveway with one bar of signal. They tap your listing or your ad, and the page sits there half-loaded. They don't wait to admire your logo. They hit back and tap the next company. Page speed isn't a technical vanity metric for a home service business; it's whether the phone number appears before the customer gives up.

Why Speed Matters More for You Than for Most Businesses

Your customers are often on a phone, often on mobile data rather than home Wi-Fi, and often in a hurry. If you run Google Ads, speed also affects what you're paying for: every click costs money whether the page loads in a moment or not at all. A slow page turns paid clicks into wasted ones, and nothing in your ad report will say "this person left because the page was slow." You just see clicks without calls.

Test It the Way a Customer Would

Skip the desktop. Do this instead:

  • Turn off Wi-Fi on your phone and open your site over mobile data, ideally from a spot with so-so signal.
  • Watch for the phone number. How long until you can read it and tap it? That moment is the one that matters, not when the last image finishes loading.
  • Try your most important page, whether that's the homepage or the page your ads point to, and then one service page.
  • Run a free tool. Google's PageSpeed Insights (search for it by name) tests any public URL and flags what's slowing it down. Look at the mobile results, and treat the list of suggestions as a guide rather than a grade to chase.
The only number worth remembering If you can't see and tap your phone number within a few seconds on mobile data, treat that as the problem to fix first, whatever the score says.

The Usual Culprits on Home Service Sites

  • Huge photos. The most common cause by far. A photo straight off a modern phone can be many times larger than a web page needs. Resize and compress images before they go on the site, and don't load a dozen of them at the top of the page.
  • Auto-playing video backgrounds. They look slick on an office monitor and drag on a phone. A still image does the job.
  • Too many plugins or add-ons. Common on DIY builders and WordPress sites that have grown over the years. Each one adds code the visitor has to download, and many were installed once and forgotten.
  • Third-party widgets. Chat popups, review sliders, booking tools, social feeds, and multiple tracking scripts all load from other servers. Keep the ones that bring in work and remove the ones nobody looks at.
  • Cheap, overloaded hosting. If every page is slow even after the images are fixed, the server itself may be the bottleneck.
  • Heavy fonts and animations. Several font families and scroll effects add weight for no help to a customer who just wants to call.

Fixes That Are Worth Doing First

In rough order of payoff for the effort:

  • Compress and resize every photo, and make sure photos lower on the page don't load until someone scrolls to them.
  • Remove widgets and plugins that don't bring in calls or leads.
  • Put the phone number and a tap-to-call button at the top, in plain text that appears right away rather than inside a slow-loading image or script.
  • Keep the page simple. A clean page with a clear headline, the phone number, a short list of services, a form, and a few real job photos loads fast almost by default.

What Not to Pay For

Be wary of anyone who sells "speed optimization" as a mysterious monthly service, or who promises a perfect score. A top score on a testing tool doesn't book jobs, and chasing the last few points can cost more than it returns. The goal is a site that feels quick on a real phone with a so-so connection. Beyond that, your money is better spent on clear service pages and a form that works.
