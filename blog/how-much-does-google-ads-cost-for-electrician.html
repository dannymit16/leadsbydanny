How Much Does Google Ads Cost for an Electrician?

Published September 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Electrical is a mid-to-high cost category in Google Ads — not quite as brutal as HVAC's peak-summer bidding wars, but not cheap either, because a panel upgrade or a service call can be worth hundreds to thousands of dollars and everyone bidding on the term knows it.

Why the Clicks Cost What They Do

Cost-per-click for electrician search terms typically runs somewhere between $10 and $35, and the spread within that range depends heavily on intent. "Electrician near me" and "power out, need electrician" sit toward the top because someone typing that is close to picking up the phone. Planned-work terms like "panel upgrade cost" or "EV charger installation" tend to cost less per click but the jobs behind them are often worth more, so they can still be a better deal even at a similar CPC. Broad terms like "electrical contractor" pull in cheaper clicks and more people who are just browsing.

What a Realistic Monthly Budget Looks Like

Split the number into two pieces, because they go to two different places: what you spend with Google for the clicks, and what you pay whoever runs the campaign.

  • Ad spend (paid to Google): A one or two-truck electrician testing the water can start around $600-1,200/month and get a real read on call volume. Companies chasing panel upgrades, EV charger installs, and generator work across a competitive metro often run $1,500-4,000+/month to stay visible on the higher-value terms.
  • Management fee (paid to whoever runs it): This is separate from ad spend. Flat monthly fees in this space usually run $500-1,500/month; agencies that bill a percentage of spend typically charge 10-20%. Know which model you're being quoted before comparing two numbers that aren't actually the same thing.
Watch for this Any package that bundles "unlimited leads" or a flat lead-guarantee into one price is either padding the ad spend to hit a number, or quietly narrowing your targeting to cheap, low-intent clicks. Ask exactly how ad spend and management fee are split before signing anything.

Cost Per Click Isn't the Number That Matters

Cost per lead is. A $20 average CPC that converts 1 in 12 clicks into a phone call is putting leads in front of you for roughly $240 each. The same $20 CPC with a faster-loading page, a visible license number, and a phone number that's impossible to miss might convert 1 in 8 instead, dropping cost per lead closer to $160. The click price is mostly outside anyone's control; what happens after the click — the page, the offer, how fast you answer — is where a well-run campaign earns its management fee.

Electrical Demand Doesn't Move Like HVAC's Does

HVAC spikes with heat waves and cold snaps. Electrical work spikes differently — storms and outages drive a fast burst of emergency search volume that can spike CPCs for a day or two in your area, then settle back down. There's also a seasonal planned-work pattern worth budgeting around: EV charger install searches have been climbing year over year, and residential holiday lighting and generator-readiness searches tend to pick up heading into November and December. A flat ad budget set in the spring won't automatically account for a storm week or the pre-holiday bump — build in some room to flex spend up when demand is actually there instead of bidding the same amount every week regardless of what's happening.
