5 Website Mistakes That Are Costing Roofing Companies Calls

Published October 3, 2026 · 5 min read

A roof is one of the most expensive, least-understood purchases a homeowner makes, and they can't judge your work by looking at it — they can only judge whether you look trustworthy. Your website is usually where that judgment gets made, often on a phone, often within a few seconds. Here are five specific problems worth checking on your own site, roughly in order of what they cost you.

1. Stock Photos Where Your Real Work Should Be

Roofing is a visual trade. A homeowner comparing three contractors will click the one that shows actual roofs on actual houses in their area — before-and-after shots, a crew on a job, a finished ridge line. Stock photos of smiling workers on a perfect roof tell them nothing, and many people can spot them. If you have phone photos from recent jobs, a simple gallery with a short caption (the roof type and the town) does more than any slogan on the page.

2. No Proof of License and Insurance

Roofing has a reputation problem: storm-chasers, door-knockers, and crews that vanish after taking a deposit. Homeowners know it, and they're looking for reassurance that you're not one of them. If your site doesn't plainly state that you're licensed where required, carry liability insurance and workers' comp, and have a real local address, you're asking them to take that on faith. Put it where people see it without hunting for it, and only state what's actually true for your business.

3. One "Roofing Services" Page for Everything

Someone searching "roof leak repair" and someone searching "metal roof installation" want different things, and so does a person with a flat commercial roof. A single page listing every service in one paragraph gives none of them a clear answer and gives Google nothing specific to match against. Separate pages for repair, full replacement, and each roofing material you actually install let each visitor land on something that speaks to their situation.

What this actually costs you None of these are redesign-level fixes. They're real photos, a few honest lines about licensing and insurance, and splitting one page into several. But a homeowner who can't see your work, can't verify you're legitimate, or can't find their specific problem is a homeowner who clicks the next result.

4. No Guidance for Storm Damage or Insurance Claims

After hail or high winds, a lot of roofing searches come from people who aren't sure whether they even have a claim. A site that explains, in plain language, what to look for, what to photograph, and what you can and can't do during the claims process gives those visitors a reason to call you first. Be careful not to promise outcomes you don't control — claim decisions belong to the insurer — but a page that honestly walks someone through the first steps builds trust fast.

5. Heavy Pages That Crawl on a Phone

Because roofing sites lean on photos, they're often the slowest sites in the home service world: huge uncompressed images, auto-playing sliders, and a gallery that loads everything at once. Someone with a leak is probably on their phone, possibly on weak signal, and won't wait. Compressed images, a tap-to-call button that stays visible, and a gallery that loads as you scroll keep the visual proof without costing you the visitor.
