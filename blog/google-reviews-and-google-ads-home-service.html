Do Google Reviews Matter for Your Ads? What Home Service Owners Should Know

Published October 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Someone searches "plumber near me," sees three companies, and picks one in a few seconds. Your ad might be one of the three. What decides whether they call you or the next company is often not the ad text at all. It's the star rating and the reviews they can see. If you're paying for clicks, reviews are part of what you're paying to be judged against.

Where Customers See Your Reviews

Reviews show up in more places than most owners realize. Your Google Business Profile shows your rating and review count in Maps and local results. Depending on the campaign type and what Google decides to show, ratings can also appear near ads. And if the customer taps through to your website, they'll judge your business again there. Even when an ad itself shows no stars, many people look you up on Maps before calling. Assume your reviews get checked.

Reviews Don't Replace Good Ads, but Weak Ones Cancel Them Out

A strong ad with a clear service, the right area, and a call button gets the click. A weak review profile can lose the call after the click. I can't give you a magic number of reviews that "unlocks" results, and nobody honest can. What I can say is what tends to hurt:

  • Very few reviews. A business with three reviews looks new or unproven next to a competitor with dozens.
  • Reviews that stopped a long time ago. If your newest review is from two years back, customers wonder whether you're still operating.
  • Unanswered complaints. One bad review matters less than a bad review with no reply. A calm, specific response shows how you handle problems.
  • A rating that doesn't match your site. If your website promises fast, careful service and your reviews say people waited days for a callback, the gap is noticeable.
A quick check before you spend on ads Search your own company name on your phone, the way a customer would. What do the first rating, the review count, and the most recent review say about you? If that screen would make you hesitate, fix it in parallel with the campaign, not after.

How to Get More Reviews Without Being Awkward

  • Ask right after the job. The customer is happiest when the problem is fixed. Ask in person, then send a text with a direct link to your review page.
  • Make it one tap. Your Google Business Profile has a link you can share. Save it on your phone so sending it takes seconds.
  • Ask every customer, not just the ones you think will say yes. It keeps the mix honest and the count growing steadily.
  • Don't pay for reviews or write your own. Google can remove fake or incentivized reviews, and customers can often tell. It isn't worth the risk to your profile.
  • Reply to every review. A short, human reply to good and bad ones shows the business is run by a real person who pays attention.

What Matters Differently by Trade

For emergency work like plumbing, HVAC, and electrical, the customer is choosing fast and leans heavily on stars and recent reviews. For roofing, landscaping, and other bigger or planned jobs, people often read more closely, so detailed reviews that mention the type of job and how the crew behaved do more work than a bare five stars. Either way, reviews that name the service ("replaced our water heater same day") help future customers picture what they'd be hiring you for.

Put Reviews Where They Help the Ad Click Pay Off

Your reviews can also live on your own site. Pull a few real, specific ones onto the pages people land on from ads, near the call button, with the customer's permission and their actual words. Don't invent or polish quotes. A site that shows genuine feedback next to a clear way to call is doing the job that the click was paid to start. For more on what the landing page needs, see Google Ads vs. local SEO, which explains how the two work together.

Should You Wait on Ads Until You Have More Reviews?

Usually not. If you have a handful of honest reviews and good work behind them, ads can run while the review count grows. If your profile has real problems, like unanswered complaints or a rating that's dropping, it's worth working on that first or at the same time, because ad clicks send more people to look at it. Ads don't create reviews. They put more eyes on whatever is already there.
