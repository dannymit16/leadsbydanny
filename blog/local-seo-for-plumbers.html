Local SEO for Plumbers: Map Pack, Reviews, and the Pages Worth Building

Published October 1, 2026 · 5 min read

For a plumber, local search is mostly a race to be one of the three businesses Google shows in the map results when someone types "plumber near me" with water on the floor. Most of the work that decides that race is unglamorous and fairly cheap. Here's where a plumbing company's effort is best spent, and what you can safely skip.

Start With Your Google Business Profile

Your Business Profile does more for local visibility than your website does, so get it right first. Pick "Plumber" as the primary category, then add the secondary categories that match work you really want, such as drain cleaning, water heater installation, or sewer line service, if they're available for your listing. Fill in every service you offer, add your hours, and make sure the name, phone number, and website match exactly what's on your site. If you work out of your house, you can hide your address and set a service area instead of showing your home to the public.

Set Your Service Area Honestly

It's tempting to list every town within an hour's drive. Google tends to favor businesses that are actually close to the searcher, so a huge service area rarely buys you rankings in places far from where you're based. List the areas you really want to work in and are willing to drive to for a typical call. You'll get better calls than you would by stretching thin.

Reviews That Name the Job

Review count and rating matter, but so does what the reviews say. A review that mentions a water heater swap or a cleared sewer line gives Google, and the next customer, a reason to connect you with that kind of work. You can't script what customers write, but you can ask at the right moment: right after a job when the problem is fixed and they're relieved. A short text with your review link works better than a card left on the counter. Reply to every review, including the bad ones, calmly and without arguing.

Don't promise what you can't deliver. If your profile or website says "24/7 emergency service," someone will test it at 2 a.m. Say what's true: if you take after-hours calls, say so clearly; if you don't, say what your hours are. A missed emergency call and a bad review cost more than the claim was worth.

Make Your Website Match Your Profile

Your site should back up what your profile says. That means your name, phone number, and service area written the same way everywhere, your license information shown where your state requires or customers expect it, and real photos of your work and your crew instead of generic stock images. Put a click-to-call number at the top of every page, since most plumbing searches happen on a phone.

Service Pages Worth Building

Build one solid page for each core service that people actually search for: water heater repair and replacement, drain cleaning, leak repair, repiping, sewer line work, and fixture installs, whichever you really do. Each page should say what the job involves, when to call, what areas you serve, and how to reach you. One useful page per service beats a dozen near-identical pages that only swap a town name.

What to Skip

  • Mass-produced town pages. Copy-and-paste pages for 30 cities add little and can look spammy. Build a page for a town only if you have something real to say about working there.
  • Directory spam. A handful of accurate listings on the major directories is enough. Dozens of low-quality ones aren't worth the time.
  • Keyword-stuffed business names. Adding "Best Cheap Plumber" to your profile name violates Google's guidelines and can get your listing suspended.

How Long It Takes

Local rankings build gradually. Cleaning up your profile can help within weeks, but earning a steady spot in the map results usually takes months of reviews and a site that's set up properly. That's one reason many plumbers run Google Ads alongside local SEO: ads can bring calls while the organic work catches up.
