Should You Put Prices on Your Home Service Website?

Published November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Almost every home service owner wonders about this at some point: if I post prices, will I scare people off, or will I stop wasting time on calls that were never going to close? There isn't one right answer for every trade, but there is a sensible way to decide. Here's how I think about it.

Why Owners Hesitate

The worry is reasonable. A posted number can be quoted back to you months later, a competitor can read it, and most jobs vary enough that one price is misleading. A roof, a panel upgrade, or a full yard redesign depends on the property, and you can't know the real number until someone has looked at it.

Why Silence Costs You Too

Visitors who find no hint of cost often assume the worst, or they call a competitor who gave them something to work with. On the other side, a business with no price signal tends to get more calls from people shopping on price alone, which means more time spent on estimates that go nowhere. A little openness can filter for the callers you actually want.

What Works Better Than a Full Price List

  • Flat-rate or fixed services, if you offer them. If you really do charge a set price for a drain clearing, a tune-up, or a standard fixture install, posting it builds trust fast. Only do this when the price is truly fixed.
  • Starting-at figures. "Service calls start at..." tells people the ballpark without promising a total. Only use a number you're willing to honor.
  • Explaining what drives the price. Size, access, materials, age of the system, permits. Owners and homeowners both appreciate a plain list of what moves a quote up or down.
  • How your estimates work. Say whether they're free, how soon you can come out, and what the customer receives afterward. This answers the real question behind "how much."

How It Differs by Trade

Repair and maintenance work such as drains, tune-ups, and fixture swaps is easiest to price publicly because the scope is usually predictable. Large projects such as re-roofs, system replacements, and landscape builds rarely are, so explaining the factors serves you better than a figure that will be wrong half the time. Emergency calls sit in between: many customers just want to know whether there's a trip charge and how it works.

A simple test Think of the last five calls you got. How many asked about price in the first minute? If it's most of them, a short price explanation on the site will save you real phone time. If almost none did, it's probably not your bottleneck.

Don't Let Prices Go Stale

If you publish a number, it needs to be right today. Costs change, and an old figure on your site creates an awkward conversation at the door. Keep prices in as few places as possible, put a date or "starting at" wording where it fits, and check them when you adjust your rates.

What About Your Ads?

The same thinking carries over to Google Ads. If your ad says "Free estimates" or "Starting at...", the landing page should back it up with the same wording. A mismatch between ad and page costs you trust and wastes the click. Whatever you decide, keep the ad, the page, and what you say on the phone consistent.

Where I Land

I wouldn't post a full price list for most home service work, but I wouldn't hide cost entirely either. Say what's fixed, give a starting point where you honestly can, and explain what changes the number and how your estimates work.
