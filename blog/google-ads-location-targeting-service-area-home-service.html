How to Set Your Google Ads Service Area So You Stop Paying for Clicks You Can't Take

Published October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Location settings are the least exciting part of a Google Ads account, and one of the easiest places to quietly burn budget. If your campaign is showing ads to people you'd never drive out to, you pay for those clicks the same as any other. Here's how I think about setting a service area properly.

Start With Where You Actually Go

Before touching the settings, write down the real list: the cities, ZIP codes, or neighborhoods where you're happy to send a truck. Then separate that into two groups. The areas you want every day, and the edges you'd take only for a bigger job or if the schedule is slow. Those edges are worth knowing about, because you may not want to pay for emergency-call clicks from there at all.

Radius vs. Named Locations

Google lets you target a radius around a point, or pick specific cities, counties, and ZIP codes. Both can work, but they fail in different ways:

  • Radius targeting is fast, but a circle ignores how people actually drive. It can pull in a neighboring town across a river or highway that's a 45-minute trip, while cutting off a suburb that's closer by road than it looks on the map.
  • Named cities and ZIP codes match how customers describe where they live, and they're easier to adjust one at a time. The trade-off is a bit more setup, and ZIP code boundaries don't always line up with your own sense of the territory.

For most home service companies I lean toward named locations built from your real service list, and then use exclusions to trim whatever still slips through.

The Setting Most People Never Check

In the campaign's location options there's a choice about who sees your ads: people who are physically in or regularly in your locations, or people in those locations plus people who show interest in them. The wider option can show your ad to someone in another state who searched for "plumber in your town," maybe a landlord, a relocating family, or just a curious searcher. For a business that only works on-site in one area, that's rarely who you want paying for. Check which option your campaign uses, and if you serve a fixed local area, the narrower presence-based setting is usually the better fit.

Where to look In the campaign settings, open Locations, then the location options. If it says presence or interest, change it to presence unless you have a specific reason to keep it.

Use Exclusions for the Places You Won't Go

You can exclude locations just as you add them. That's useful when:

  • A neighboring town sits inside your radius but is outside your real territory.
  • One area keeps sending calls you turn down, whether it's too far, too small a job, or outside what you cover.
  • You want to separate a profitable core area from the edges by running them as different campaigns with different budgets.

Splitting your core area from the outer fringe is often better than excluding the fringe entirely. It lets you give the near-in customers most of the budget and treat the farther ones as a smaller experiment.

How to Tell If It's Working

Two places answer this. The location report in Google Ads shows where clicks and calls are coming from by area, so you can spot a town eating budget without producing jobs. And your own call log is the real test: if you keep hearing "do you service my area?" and the answer is no, your targeting is too wide or your ad isn't saying where you work. Putting your service area right in the ad text and on the landing page helps people screen themselves out before they click.

Don't Go Too Narrow, Either

It's possible to over-correct. Tight targeting in a small town can leave you with so few searches that the campaign barely runs. If volume is thin, widen the area a step at a time and watch what happens to call quality, instead of flipping the whole setting at once. Also remember that emergency searches and project searches behave differently: someone with no hot water will accept a wider driving range from you than a homeowner just collecting quotes.

Where I Land

Build the target from your real service list, switch to presence-based targeting if you work on-site only, exclude the places you won't go, and review the location report every so often. I build every site and run every campaign myself, so these settings get checked by the same person who set them up. You can cancel any time in the first 30 days.
