Should You Bid on Your Own Company Name in Google Ads?

Published October 28, 2026 · 5 min read

You search your own company name on your phone and see an ad for your business sitting right above your free listing. Or worse, you see a competitor's ad there. Either way, the question comes up: should you be paying Google for clicks from people who were already looking for you? The honest answer is "sometimes," and it's worth knowing which side of that you're on.

What Bidding on Your Own Name Means

A branded search is anyone typing your company name, or something close to it, into Google. Bidding on it means running an ad that shows when they do. These people already know who you are, so they tend to call or fill out a form at a higher rate than someone who searched "plumber near me." That's exactly why the numbers can look great and still be misleading: you may have gotten that call anyway.

When It's Worth Paying For

  • A competitor is bidding on your name. Search your company name on your phone. If another company's ad shows above your listing, a sliver of your own customers may be clicking it. A cheap branded ad can keep that spot.
  • Your free listing is weak or buried. If your website doesn't show first, or lead-gen directories and review sites outrank you for your own name, a branded ad puts you back on top.
  • You have a specific offer or message. A branded ad can send people straight to the right page, such as your financing details or your emergency line, rather than leaving them to find it on their own.
  • You want clean tracking. Separating branded from non-branded campaigns shows how much of your ad performance is people who already knew you.

When It Mostly Wastes Money

If you rank first for your own name, nobody else is bidding on it, and your listing already has your phone number and reviews showing, paying for the same click may only change who gets billed. Some of those callers would have clicked the free result. The risk is subtler than the cost: branded clicks are cheap and convert well, so they can make an account's overall numbers look healthier than the campaigns bringing in brand-new customers.

A five-minute check Search your exact company name on your phone, then again with a common misspelling. Look at who shows above you and what the top of the page offers a caller. If it's just you, with your number visible, there's little to protect. If it's someone else, that's your answer.

Keep Branded and Non-Branded Separate

If you do run branded ads, keep them in their own campaign with their own budget. Mixed together, cheap branded clicks blur the picture of what your "plumber," "AC repair," or "roof replacement" campaigns are really producing. Owners often judge their ads by the total number of calls, and branded calls can quietly inflate that total. Ask whoever manages your account to show the two sets of numbers apart.

What About Bidding on Competitors' Names?

That's a different decision. Clicks on a rival's name tend to cost more, the searcher was looking for someone else, and the ad policies limit what you can say. It can make sense in narrow cases, but for most small home service companies the budget does more work on searches for the actual service in your area. I'd get those right first.
