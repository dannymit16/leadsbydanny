5 Google Ads Mistakes That Are Wasting Electricians' Ad Budget

Published October 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Electrical work is a wide range of jobs under one search term. A flickering-light call, a panel upgrade, and a whole-house rewire all start with someone typing "electrician near me," but they are very different jobs to win. That's why electrician campaigns tend to leak money in specific ways. Here are five of the most common, and how to fix each.

1. Sending Every Click to the Homepage

Someone who searched "panel upgrade" wants to see that you do panel upgrades. If the ad drops them on a homepage that lists ten services, they have to hunt, and many won't. Each major service you advertise (service calls and repairs, panel upgrades, EV charger installs, generators, rewiring) should send people to a page about exactly that job, with your phone number and a request form visible without scrolling.

2. One Budget for Small Jobs and Big Ones

An outlet or switch repair and a full panel replacement are not worth the same to you, so they shouldn't compete for the same pool of money. When everything shares one budget and one set of keywords, the cheap, high-volume searches tend to eat the spend while the higher-value jobs get whatever is left. Separate campaigns for your most profitable work let you decide how much each type of job deserves, rather than letting Google's averages decide for you.

Ask which jobs you actually want If a small-repair call costs you a truck roll and an hour, and a panel upgrade fills a day, your ad budget should reflect that. Decide what you want more of, then build the campaigns around it.

3. Paying for Job Seekers and DIYers

Not everyone searching an electrical term wants to hire an electrician. Some are looking for work ("electrician jobs," "apprenticeship"), some are checking pay ("electrician salary"), and some want to do the work themselves ("how to wire an outlet," "wire gauge chart"). Without negative keywords to block those searches, you pay for clicks that were never going to call. Reviewing the search terms report regularly and adding negatives is one of the least glamorous and most valuable habits in an electrician's account.

4. Running Emergency Ads With No After-Hours Plan

"Emergency electrician" searches come from people with sparking outlets, dead power, or a tripped main, and they call the first business that picks up. If your ads run at 11 p.m. and the call goes to a voicemail nobody checks until morning, that click was wasted and a competitor probably got the job. Either schedule emergency ads for the hours you can actually answer, or make sure there is a real plan for who takes those calls. Don't promise 24/7 response in an ad if you can't deliver it.

5. Not Knowing Which Calls Came From Ads

Electricians get a lot of their leads by phone, and a campaign that only tracks form fills misses most of the picture. If you can't tell which ads and keywords produced calls, and which of those calls turned into booked jobs, you're guessing about what's working. Call tracking plus a simple habit of noting which calls became jobs lets you cut what doesn't produce and put more behind what does.

The Pattern Behind All Five

Most of these come down to the same thing: the campaign was set up once and then left alone. Electrician searches are specific enough that a campaign matched to your real services, your real hours, and your real service area will almost always beat a broad one, and it takes regular attention to keep it that way.
