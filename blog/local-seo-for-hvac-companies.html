Local SEO for HVAC Companies: What's Worth Doing, What Isn't

Published September 21, 2026 · 6 min read

HVAC is a different animal than most home service trades because the same customer searches for two completely different things depending on the season and the moment: "AC repair near me" at 4pm on a 102-degree day, and "furnace installation cost" while calmly comparing quotes in October. Local SEO that ignores this split wastes effort. Here's what actually matters, and what you can skip.

Pick the Right Google Business Profile Categories

Most HVAC companies set their primary category as "HVAC contractor" and stop there. Add every secondary category you actually do — Air conditioning repair service, Air conditioning contractor, Furnace repair service, Heating contractor, Duct cleaning service — because Google uses these to decide which searches your listing is eligible to show up for. A company set up only as "HVAC contractor" can lose visibility on the narrower, high-intent searches like "furnace repair" even though that's exactly the job they want. List your actual service area, not a blanket radius that includes towns you don't really cover, and if you're EPA 608 certified or hold a manufacturer certification (Trane Comfort Specialist, Carrier Factory Authorized, etc.), that's worth a line in your business description — homeowners comparing two similar-looking companies notice it.

Build Content Around the Season You're Actually In

This is where HVAC diverges most from a trade like roofing. Search volume for AC repair spikes hard in June through August and nearly disappears in winter; furnace and heating searches do the reverse. If your website and Google posts stay static year-round, you're leaving relevance on the table for half the year. A simple habit: post a Google Business Profile update or a short blog note tied to what's actually happening — AC tune-up reminders in May, furnace safety checks in October, freeze-warning emergency availability in a cold snap. It doesn't need to be elaborate. It needs to match what people are searching for that week, which a "set it and forget it" website never does.

Time Your Review Requests to the Right Job

Emergency repair customers and install/maintenance-plan customers behave differently after the job. Someone whose AC got fixed on a 100-degree day is often willing to leave a review immediately out of sheer relief — ask before you leave the driveway, by text with a direct link. Install and maintenance-plan customers are a slower burn; the best moment is usually a few days later, once the system's been running and they've noticed it works and the house is comfortable, not mid-installation when there's still dust in the hallway. A steady trickle from both groups, a few reviews a month, does more for local ranking than a one-time push to blast out review requests to your whole customer list at once.

NAP Consistency and Licensing Info

Your name, address, and phone number need to match exactly across your website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Angi, HomeAdvisor, and the Better Business Bureau. HVAC has an added wrinkle: many states require a visible license number on your website and marketing, and a mismatched or missing license number is both a trust signal problem and, in some states, a compliance issue. Check what your state actually requires and make sure it's the same number everywhere it appears.

Service-Area Pages: Split Install From Repair Intent

If you cover several towns, a page per town can help, but only with genuinely different content per page — and for HVAC specifically, it's worth separating repair-intent content from install-intent content rather than mixing both into one generic "HVAC services in [town]" page. Someone searching "AC not cooling [town]" wants speed and availability messaging; someone searching "new furnace installation [town]" wants pricing ranges, financing, and brand info. Cramming both into one thin page serves neither search well and doesn't perform as well as fewer, more targeted pages.

What's a waste of money in 2026 Buying reviews, keyword-stuffed footer text, and paying for a dozen thin service-area pages that all say the same thing with the town name swapped. Also skip any agency that can't explain which of your GBP categories they set and why — if they can't answer that in one sentence, they haven't actually looked at your listing.

What This Actually Costs You

The categories, the seasonal posting habit, the review timing, the NAP cleanup — all of that is time, not money. Well-built service-area pages take longer and are a fair thing to pay someone for if you'd rather not build them yourself. A correctly categorized Business Profile with a steady review habit will outperform an expensive "SEO package" that skipped the fundamentals.

How Long Before You See Anything

A properly categorized Google Business Profile with a handful of fresh reviews can start showing up in the local map pack within weeks. Seasonal content pays off gradually and compounds — the furnace post you write this October helps you again next October. Service-area pages take longer to earn trust with Google. Nobody can hand you a guaranteed date or ranking; anyone who does is guessing. What you control is doing the fundamentals consistently through both halves of the HVAC calendar instead of only thinking about it when call volume drops.
