DIY Website Builder vs. Custom Site: What Roofing Companies Actually Need

Published October 9, 2026 · 6 min read

If you run a roofing company and you're shopping for a website, the first question isn't which company to hire. It's whether a builder like Wix or Squarespace is enough, or whether a custom-built site is worth paying for. Both are legitimate. The right answer depends on what you need the site to do, and that matters more for roofers than most trades, because a roofing customer is about to make one of the biggest purchases of their year and wants proof before they call.

What a DIY Builder Does Well

Builders are good at getting a clean page live fast and cheap with no technical skill. If you're a small crew that gets nearly all its work from referrals and yard signs, and you mainly need a place where someone can confirm you exist, see your phone number, and read a few reviews, a builder site can do that job. Pick a template, replace the stock photos with pictures from your own jobs, add your number, and you're live in an afternoon.

Where Roofing Makes the Gap Show Up

The trouble starts when the site has to earn leads. Roofing searches tend to be specific: roof replacement, leak repair, storm damage, an inspection before selling a house. Those are different jobs with different worries behind them. A template gives you one homepage and maybe a generic services page. Getting a separate, properly built page for each of those jobs inside a builder's editor is possible, but it's clunky, and most owners never get around to it.

Proof is the other issue. Roofing buyers want to see finished roofs, your license and insurance, and any manufacturer certifications you hold, and they want it in front of them quickly on a phone. Templates are laid out for the widest possible audience, so that proof usually ends up buried or missing. Builder pages are also often heavy under the hood, and a gallery of large job photos on a bloated page is slow to load on a phone with weak signal, which is exactly where a homeowner with a leak is standing.

Storm Season and Ads Raise the Stakes

After a hail or wind event, a lot of people search at once and call the first few roofers who look credible. A slow page or a hard-to-find phone number costs you that window. If you're also paying for Google Ads, the cost is direct: you pay for the click whether or not the page turns it into a call. A landing page built around one job, with your number, service area, and proof visible right away, gives the click a much better chance than a general-purpose homepage.

The real question is what the site's job is. If it only needs to exist so people can verify you're real, a builder is often enough. If it's supposed to bring in estimates for roof replacements and repairs, especially with ad spend behind it, it should be built for that job specifically.

What "Custom" Doesn't Have to Mean

Custom doesn't have to mean a huge agency project with a long timeline. For most roofing companies it means a site built around your services and your service area, with fast loading, a layout that works on a phone, tap-to-call, working forms, and clean page structure handled correctly from the start, instead of you guessing at settings in a template editor.

A Reasonable Way to Decide

If you're just starting out or your lead volume from any channel is close to zero, a cheap builder site is a defensible first step. If you already get steady work from referrals and want a dependable stream of new estimate requests, particularly if Google Ads is part of the plan, a site built around that goal will usually earn back the price difference through calls a slow, generic page would have lost.
