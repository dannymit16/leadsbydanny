DIY Website Builder vs. Custom Site: What Electricians Actually Need

Published October 16, 2026 · 6 min read

If you run an electrical business and you're shopping for a website, the first question isn't who to hire. It's whether a builder like Wix or Squarespace is enough, or whether a custom-built site is worth paying for. Both are legitimate options. The right one depends on what you need the site to do, and electricians have a particular challenge: you're asking people to let you into their walls and their panel, so the site has to earn trust fast, and it has to sort very different customers at the same time.

What a DIY Builder Does Well

Builders are good at getting a clean page live quickly and cheaply with no technical skill. If most of your work comes from repeat customers, builders, and referrals, and you mainly need somewhere people can confirm you're real, see your phone number, and read a few reviews, a builder site can do that. Pick a template, swap in photos of your own work, add your number, and you can be live in an afternoon.

Where Electrical Work Makes the Gap Show Up

The trouble starts when the site has to bring in new customers. Electrical searches are specific: panel upgrade, EV charger installation, ceiling fan or lighting install, rewiring, an outlet that stopped working. Those are different jobs with different customers and different worries. A template hands you one homepage and a generic services page. You can build separate pages for each job inside a builder, but it's slow, the editor fights you, and most owners never finish.

Trust signals are the second gap. Careful customers look for your license, proof of insurance, and real photos of finished work, like a tidy panel or a clean install. Templates are laid out for the broadest possible audience, so that proof often ends up buried below stock imagery. Builder pages can also be heavy under the hood, and a slow page is a real problem when the person on the other end is standing in a dark kitchen searching on a phone.

Emergency Calls and Scheduled Jobs Need Different Paths

Someone with a dead outlet or a tripped breaker that won't reset wants to see a phone number right now. Someone planning a panel upgrade or an EV charger wants to read about the process, see your license, and request a quote. A general-purpose template puts both people on the same page and makes neither of them happy. A site built for electricians puts a tap-to-call button within reach on every screen and gives the planned jobs their own pages with a simple quote request.

The real question is what the site's job is. If it only needs to exist so people can verify you're legitimate, a builder is often enough. If it's supposed to bring in new panel, charger, and repair calls, especially with ad spend behind it, it should be built for that job specifically.

When Google Ads Are in the Picture

If you're paying for Google Ads, every click costs money whether or not the page turns it into a call. Sending an "EV charger installation" click to a generic homepage makes the visitor hunt for the right information, and many won't. A page built around that one job, with your number, service area, and license visible right away, gives the click a much better chance of becoming a call.

What "Custom" Doesn't Have to Mean

Custom doesn't have to mean a huge agency project with a long timeline. For most electrical contractors it means a site built around your services and your service area, with fast loading, a layout that works on a phone, tap-to-call, working forms, and clean page structure handled correctly from the start instead of you guessing at settings in a template editor.

A Reasonable Way to Decide

If you're just starting out and your lead volume is close to zero, a cheap builder site is a defensible first step. If you already get steady work from referrals and want a dependable flow of new calls and quote requests, particularly if Google Ads is part of the plan, a site built around that goal will usually earn back the price difference through calls a slow, generic page would have lost.
