How Much Does a Website Cost for a Plumbing Company?

Published August 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: anywhere from $0 to $10,000+, depending on who builds it. That range is useless on its own, so here's what actually determines where you land — and what matters more than the price tag.

The Three Real Options

DIY builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy) — $15-40/month. Fine if you have hours to spend learning a page builder and don't mind a site that looks like every other DIY plumbing site. The real cost isn't the subscription, it's your time.

Freelancers — $300-1,500 one-time. Wide range in quality. Some are excellent, some disappear after taking a deposit. Ask to see live sites they've built for other trades before paying anything.

Agencies — $2,000-10,000+. You're paying for a team, an office, and often a salesperson between you and whoever actually builds the site. Not always worth it for a single-location plumbing business.

What actually gets you calls A $5,000 site with a slow-loading homepage and a buried phone number will lose to a $500 site that loads in under a second and has a click-to-call button above the fold. Price doesn't determine performance — build quality does.

What a Plumbing Site Actually Needs

  • Loads fast on a phone. Most searches for "plumber near me" happen on mobile, often from someone standing next to a leaking pipe. Every extra second of load time costs you calls.
  • A phone number that's impossible to miss. Not buried in a footer — visible the second the page loads, tappable on mobile.
  • Clear service area and services. People bounce fast if they can't tell in five seconds whether you cover their zip code and their specific problem.
  • Real trust signals. Licensed/insured badges, years in business, and honest pricing beat stock photos of smiling plumbers every time.

The Hidden Costs Nobody Mentions

Hosting, a domain name, and ongoing maintenance add up regardless of who builds the site — budget $10-20/month for that no matter what. And if you're planning to run Google Ads to the site, that's a separate, ongoing budget from the site build itself — anyone who bundles "unlimited leads" into a flat website fee is either losing money or cutting corners on your ad spend.
