Local SEO for Roofing Companies: What Actually Moves the Needle

Published August 24, 2026 · 5 min read

"Local SEO" gets sold to roofers as a mysterious, ongoing service worth $1,000-2,000 a month. Some of what falls under that label genuinely matters. A lot of it is padding to justify the retainer. Here's what's actually worth your time, roughly in order of impact.

Start With Your Google Business Profile

Before you touch your website, claim and fully fill out your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) — it's free, and for local trades it often matters more than the website itself. Pick the most accurate primary category ("Roofing contractor," not just "Contractor"), add secondary categories for anything else you genuinely do (gutter installation, storm damage repair), list every service you actually offer, and set your real service areas rather than a generic 50-mile radius. Upload real photos of your crews and finished jobs — before-and-afters do well — not stock images of roofers who don't work for you. Answer the Q&A section yourself before someone posts a wrong answer for you, and post an update or a recent job photo every few weeks so the listing doesn't look abandoned. This takes an afternoon to set up properly, not a month.

Reviews Beat Almost Everything Else

Review count and recency are one of the strongest local ranking signals Google uses, and they're the first thing a homeowner checks before calling anyone. Build a habit: every time a job wraps and the customer's happy, ask for a review on the spot — send a direct link by text, don't make them hunt for your listing. A steady trickle of 2-3 real reviews a month beats a burst of 20 fake ones, and buying reviews is a fast way to get your listing suspended.

NAP Consistency Isn't Exciting, But It's Foundational

NAP means Name, Address, Phone — and it needs to match, exactly, everywhere your business is listed: your website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Angi, BBB, Nextdoor, Facebook. If your Google listing says "Suite 200" and your website says "Ste 200," that's a small inconsistency Google's systems notice. Spend an hour auditing your top 10-15 listings and fixing mismatches. It's boring work and it's foundational — skipping it undermines everything else you do.

Service-Area Pages, Done Right

If you cover multiple towns or a whole county, a single page per town can help — but only if each page has genuinely different content: specific neighborhoods you work in, local permit or HOA quirks, actual project photos from that area. Copy-pasting one page fifteen times and swapping the town name is a well-known trick, Google recognizes the pattern, and thin duplicate pages can actively hurt you rather than help. Better to have five well-built area pages than twenty thin ones.

What's a waste of money in 2026 Keyword-stuffed page footers, paid backlinks from link farms, and any agency promising "guaranteed #1 ranking" or a specific position by a specific date. Nobody controls Google's algorithm well enough to guarantee a ranking — anyone who says they can is selling you a story, not a result.

What This Actually Costs You

Most of the highest-leverage local SEO work — the Business Profile, review requests, NAP cleanup — costs time, not money. Service-area pages and ongoing content take longer, and that's where it's fair to pay someone if you'd rather not do it yourself. Just know what you're paying for: a well-built site with a solid Google Business Profile and a steady review habit will outperform a flashy $2,000/month "SEO package" with none of the fundamentals in place.

How Long Before You See Anything

Realistically, weeks to a few months, and it depends heavily on how much competition you're up against in your area. A Google Business Profile filled out correctly can start showing up in the local "map pack" fairly quickly once you have a handful of reviews. Website content and service-area pages take longer to earn trust with Google. Nobody honest can hand you a date or a guaranteed position — anyone who does is guessing, not measuring. What you can control is doing the fundamentals consistently instead of chasing whatever tactic an ad promised would fix it overnight.
