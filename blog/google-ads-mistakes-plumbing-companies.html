5 Google Ads Mistakes That Are Wasting Plumbing Companies' Ad Budget

Published September 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Plumbing search traffic is a mix of people in genuine trouble and people who are just curious, and a Google Ads account that treats every click the same will waste money on both ends. Here are five mistakes I see most often in plumbing accounts, and what to do about each one.

Emergency Clicks and Routine Clicks Are Getting Treated the Same

"Burst pipe" and "no hot water" are not the same kind of search as "drain cleaning cost" or "install new faucet." The first two come from someone who wants a truck moving in the next hour; the second two come from someone comparing options over a few days. Running them through the same ad group with the same bid and the same generic "Licensed Plumber — Call Now" copy means you're either overpaying for routine jobs or underbidding the emergency clicks that would actually justify a premium bid. Separating emergency keywords into their own campaign with copy that leads with "answering now" and a bid strategy built to win those auctions fixes both sides of that problem at once.

DIY Search Terms Are Bleeding Your Budget

Plumbing has some of the heaviest DIY search volume of any home service trade. Broad match on terms like "leak repair" or "toilet running" pulls in "how to fix a running toilet myself," "snake a drain video," and "plumber's putty vs silicone" right alongside the people who actually want a professional. None of those searchers are calling you. A negative keyword list built around "how to," "diy," "video," "vs," and specific tool or product names is a short project that stops a real share of wasted spend, and it's worth revisiting every couple of months as new DIY terms show up in your search terms report.

Bids Don't Move After Hours

A decent share of real plumbing emergencies happen nights and weekends, and that's exactly when a lot of homeowners assume no one will pick up — so fewer companies bid aggressively then, which makes it cheaper and easier to win those clicks if your account is actually set up to compete for them. An account running flat bids around the clock either misses that window entirely or, more often, isn't running ad scheduling adjustments at all and has no idea the opportunity exists. If a call after 9pm on a Saturday is worth more to you than a Tuesday afternoon estimate request, your bids should say so.

Watch for this Ask whoever manages your account whether ad scheduling and bid adjustments by time of day are turned on, and when they were last reviewed. "We haven't touched it" is a common answer, and it's a sign the account is running on autopilot rather than being actively managed.

Blending an $89 Drain Cleaning With a $6,000 Repipe Into One Number

If your reporting shows a single blended cost-per-lead across every service you offer, it's hiding more than it's telling you. A drain cleaning lead and a whole-house repipe lead are worth wildly different amounts, and averaging them together makes a campaign look worse — or better — than it actually is. A cost-per-lead that looks high on paper might be perfectly efficient if a chunk of those leads are turning into big-ticket repipe or water heater replacement jobs, but a single blended figure won't show you that. Breaking results out by service type is the only way to know which keywords are actually paying for themselves.

One Landing Page for a Leaking Faucet and a Full Repipe

Someone with a leaking faucet wants to know you can get someone out today and roughly what a repair like that costs. Someone considering a whole-house repipe or water heater replacement wants to see your licensing, warranty terms, and maybe financing before they'll call — it's a bigger decision made over more time. Sending both of those clicks to the same generic homepage asks one page to do two very different jobs, and it usually does neither one well. Even a simple split — one page built around fast response for repairs and emergencies, another built around trust and planning for bigger installs — tends to convert noticeably better than one page trying to cover everything.
