5 Website Mistakes That Are Costing Electricians Calls

Published August 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Most electrical contractor websites aren't bad because the design is ugly. They're bad because of a handful of specific, fixable problems that quietly send people to the next name on the search results page. Here's what to check on your own site, in order of how much it actually costs you.

1. No Mention of Emergency Work Above the Fold

A big share of electrical searches are urgent — a breaker that won't stop tripping, a panel that's smoking, an outlet that sparked. If someone lands on your homepage and has to scroll or hunt to find out whether you do same-day or after-hours work, they've probably already opened a second tab. If you offer emergency service, say so in the first screen someone sees, not buried in a paragraph three sections down.

2. The Phone Number Isn't Actually Clickable

This sounds too basic to matter, and yet it's one of the most common things wrong with contractor sites. On mobile, a phone number that's just styled text instead of a real tel: link means someone has to copy it, switch apps, and dial manually. That's friction most people won't push through. Test it yourself on your phone right now — tap your own number and see if it dials.

3. No Proof of License and Insurance

Electrical work is one of the categories where people are actively worried about hiring someone unlicensed or uninsured — a bad wiring job is a fire risk, not just an inconvenience. If your license number and insurance status aren't visible somewhere obvious, you're leaving out the exact detail that turns a hesitant visitor into a caller. It doesn't need to be fancy — a line in the header or footer with your license number is enough.

What this actually costs you If your site gets a few hundred visits a month and even a modest share of them bounce because of friction like this, that's real jobs going to a competitor whose site made it one step easier to call. None of these fixes require a redesign — most are an afternoon of edits.

4. Slow Load Times on Mobile

A lot of contractor sites are built on page builders loaded with animations, oversized images, and tracking scripts that add up. If your homepage takes more than two or three seconds to load on a phone with average signal, you're losing people before they see anything — including the emergency messaging and the phone number you just fixed. Run your own site through Google's PageSpeed Insights and see the mobile score. If it's in the 30s or 40s, that's a real problem, not a nitpick.

5. Vague Service Areas and Service Lists

"Proudly serving the greater metro area" tells a visitor nothing. People want to know fast whether you cover their specific city or zip code, and whether you handle their specific problem — panel upgrades, EV charger installs, ceiling fan wiring, whatever brought them to search in the first place. A short, specific list beats a vague paragraph every time, and it also happens to help you show up for more specific searches.
