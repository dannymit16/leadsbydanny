Who Owns Your Website and Domain? What Home Service Owners Should Check Before Hiring a Web Builder

Published October 31, 2026 · 5 min read

Most owners judge a website on how it looks. The part that can hurt you later is less visible: who actually controls it. If your web builder registered your domain in their own account, or the site lives on hosting only they can log into, you may be renting something you thought you bought. That usually stays invisible until you want to switch builders, and then it gets expensive or messy.

Three Things You Should Control

  • The domain name. This is your web address, like yourcompany.com. It's registered through a registrar, and the registrant is the legal owner. It should be you or your business, with an email address you can access.
  • The hosting. This is the server where the site's files live. You don't have to manage it yourself, but you should know who the provider is and how a handoff would work.
  • The site itself. The pages, text, photos, and code. Ask whether you get a copy if you leave, or whether it only works on the builder's platform.
A two-minute check you can do today Search your domain in a public WHOIS lookup. Many registrars hide personal details for privacy, but you can often still see the registrar and the registration and expiration dates. Then try to log into the registrar account that holds it. If you don't know which account that is, find out now, not during an emergency.

Why This Matters for a Home Service Business

Your domain isn't just a website address. It's tied to your Google Business Profile, your business email, the links on your ads, and every directory listing that points to you. If a builder holds the domain and disappears, stops responding, or sends a surprise bill, you can lose the address customers already know. Starting over on a new domain means rebuilding that trust from scratch, and any search visibility you built goes with it.

Questions to Ask Before You Pay Anyone

  • Whose name will the domain be registered under? The right answer is yours. If you already own a domain, ask how the builder will use it without taking control of it.
  • Who pays for hosting, and is there a monthly fee? Hosting and a domain are normal ongoing costs. What you want to avoid is a vague monthly fee with no explanation of what it covers.
  • Can I get a copy of the site if I leave? Some website builders let you export your pages, and some don't. Get the answer in writing, before you commit.
  • What happens if I want to cancel? A clear exit path is a good sign. A long contract or an unclear shutdown is not.
  • Who has the logins? You should know where your registrar and hosting accounts are and be able to reach them without asking permission.

Problems That Come Up Again and Again

None of these require a bad actor. They often come from a builder who set things up quickly and never mentioned it. Common ones include a domain registered in the builder's personal account, a renewal notice going to an old email nobody reads, a site on a platform with no export option, and a domain that expires because nobody was watching the date. An expired domain can be picked up by someone else, and getting it back may not be possible.

Protect Yourself, Whoever You Hire

  • Put the domain in your own registrar account and turn on auto-renew.
  • Use an email address that belongs to the business, not one tied to a former employee or a builder.
  • Keep a simple note listing your registrar, hosting provider, and where each login lives.
  • Turn on two-step verification for the registrar account.
  • Look at the renewal date once a year, and keep the payment card current.
