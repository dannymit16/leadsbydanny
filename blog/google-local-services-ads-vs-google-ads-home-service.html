Google Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads: Which Fits a Home Service Business?

Published October 20, 2026 · 5 min read

If you've looked into Google for your trade, you've probably noticed two different things at the top of the results: the regular ads, and a separate box of companies with a green check and the words "Google Guaranteed" or "Google Screened." Those are Local Services Ads, and they work differently enough from regular Google Ads that choosing between them is a real decision, not a setting. Here's how to think it through without anyone selling you on either one.

The Core Difference: Paying for Leads vs. Paying for Clicks

With regular Google Ads, you pay when someone clicks your ad, whether or not they call. With Local Services Ads, you generally pay for a lead — typically a call or message from a customer asking about your service — rather than for the click. The price per lead depends on your trade and your market, so the only honest number is the one you see in your own account.

That sounds like an easy win for LSA, and for some businesses it is. But the two programs also differ in how much control you get, and control is where most of the tradeoffs live.

How the Two Compare

  • Setup. Local Services Ads involve a verification process — things like license, insurance, and background checks, depending on the category and area. Regular Google Ads need a payment method and a plan, not paperwork.
  • Where you show up. LSA listings sit above the regular ads for the searches they cover. Regular ads can appear on a wider range of searches, including ones LSA doesn't offer for your trade or area.
  • Your website. A regular ad sends someone to a page you control, so your site does a lot of the work. An LSA mostly sells on your profile — reviews, rating, hours, service area — and the customer may never see your site before calling.
  • Control. With regular ads you choose keywords, write the ad text, pick the page, and block searches you don't want. With LSA, Google decides much more about when and how you appear.
  • Reviews. Both benefit from good reviews, but LSA leans on them heavily because the profile is most of what the customer sees.
The question that decides it Do you want to be a name on a list of verified companies, or do you want to control the message and the page people land on? LSA favors the first; regular Google Ads favor the second. Many established companies end up running both.

When Local Services Ads Tend to Fit

  • You're licensed and insured, and your paperwork is easy to produce.
  • You already have a solid number of recent reviews and a good rating.
  • Your work is the kind people want booked fast — a leak, a dead AC, a tripped panel — and a trust badge helps them pick quickly.
  • You can answer the phone, because a lead you pay for and miss is money gone.

When Regular Google Ads Tend to Fit

  • You sell larger or more specific jobs, like replacements, remodels, or full installs, where you want to speak to that exact need.
  • You want to target particular services, neighborhoods, or times of year instead of taking whatever the program sends.
  • Your trade or area isn't well covered by LSA categories.
  • You want your website to carry the persuasion — photos of your work, pricing context, service pages, a clear quote form.

Mistakes to Avoid With Either One

  • Paying for leads you never check. Look at what came in. Wrong-service or out-of-area leads are worth disputing where the program allows it.
  • Letting calls go to voicemail. A paid lead that rolls to a mailbox usually calls the next company.
  • Treating a budget as a promise. A small budget in a competitive trade can mean very few leads either way. Ask what a realistic month looks like before committing.
  • Ignoring the website. Even if LSA brings the first call, people still search your name afterward. A thin or outdated site can undo the trust the badge earned.
  • Not tracking results. Whichever you use, count booked jobs, not just leads.
