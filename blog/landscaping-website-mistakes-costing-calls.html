5 Website Mistakes That Are Costing Landscaping Companies Calls

Published October 5, 2026 · 5 min read

Landscaping is a visual trade. People hire you based on what your work looks like, and most of them judge that from a phone before they ever call. Here are five website mistakes that quietly cost landscaping companies leads, and what to do about each.

1. No Real Photos of Your Own Work

Stock photos of perfectly manicured lawns tell a visitor nothing about you. Real before-and-after shots of your own jobs do the selling that a paragraph of copy can't. You don't need a professional shoot; clear, well-lit phone photos of finished work, with a line about what the job was, beat stock images every time.

2. One Page for Every Service

A weekly mowing customer, someone planning a patio and retaining wall, and a person who needs a spring cleanup are looking for different things. Cramming maintenance, hardscaping, planting, and irrigation onto one page makes each one thin. Give your main services their own pages so a visitor can land on exactly what they came for.

Maintenance and projects are different buyers Recurring maintenance is a small, steady decision. A design-build project is a big one that people research for weeks. If your site speaks to both with the same words, it probably persuades neither.

3. No Seasonal Messaging

Landscaping demand moves with the calendar: spring cleanups, summer maintenance, fall leaf removal, and winter planning for next year's projects. A site that never changes can look like it's about a different company than the one working in your area right now. Even a short, current line near the top about what you're booking this season helps a visitor feel the business is active.

4. An Unclear Service Area

Landscaping crews are travel-bound, and most owners have a radius where the numbers stop working. If your site doesn't say where you work, you get calls you can't take and miss the ones you can. List the towns you actually cover, in plain words, so the right people call and the wrong ones don't.

5. Heavy Images That Crawl on a Phone

Because landscaping sites lean on photos, they're often the slowest ones around. Full-resolution images straight off a phone can make a page take forever to load on a cellular connection, and visitors leave before the gallery appears. Photos should be resized and compressed so the page loads quickly without losing the look of the work.

Make It Easy to Reach You

Whatever else your site does, a visitor should be able to tap your phone number or send a quick request without hunting for it. If someone is ready to ask for an estimate, don't make them scroll to find out how.
